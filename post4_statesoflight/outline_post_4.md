# States of Light

*What lasers teach us about light—and how that lets us hear gravitational waves.*

## Editorial note

Working title and subtitle agreed on 19 October 2026. The subtitle promises a substantive discussion of squeezed light in gravitational-wave interferometers such as LIGO, not merely a passing mention.

## Pixar Story Spine — narrative arc (working draft)

**Once upon a time…** Scientists described light using two complementary pictures: waves and particles.

**Every day…** Those pictures explained many experiments, but neither picture on its own fully described the quantum state of a light field.

**Until one day…** The laser was invented, producing light that behaved remarkably like a classical electromagnetic wave. Roy Glauber helped answer a new question: what quantum state describes such light?

**Because of that…** Coherent states gave a quantum description of classical-looking laser light. In phase space, the classical point becomes a small uncertainty region: even laser light has quantum fluctuations.

**Because of that…** Comparing coherent light with thermal light and photon-number states revealed that quantum uncertainty can take different forms. This raised a new possibility: could its distribution be deliberately reshaped?

**Until finally…** Squeezed states showed how to reduce fluctuations in one field quadrature while increasing them in the conjugate quadrature.

**And ever since then…** Squeezed light has been used to improve the sensitivity of gravitational-wave detectors such as LIGO, helping us observe tiny distortions of spacetime.

### Editorial through-line

From **understanding the quantum state of apparently classical laser light** to **engineering quantum uncertainty for gravitational-wave detection**. Reuse the phase-space picture throughout: classical point → coherent-state uncertainty disk → contrasts with thermal/number states → squeezed ellipse → LIGO measurement noise.

### Physics and scope notes

- Recall the annihilation operator from post two: `a |α⟩ = α |α⟩`. Coherent states are its eigenstates, not eigenstates of the creation operator.
- Keep thermal and photon-number states concise, as contrasts rather than full treatments; earlier posts already discussed number states.
- Avoid implying that Glauber himself invented squeezing or that a real laser always emits a pure coherent state.
- Treat LIGO as a substantive payoff: squeezed vacuum at the interferometer output, relevant quadratures, and the frequency-dependent trade-off between shot noise and radiation-pressure noise.
- The historical introduction is a storytelling frame, not a claim that wave–particle complementarity was scientifically superseded by the laser.
